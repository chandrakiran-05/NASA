## Original concept

**Orbital Folio:** a planetary exhibit that opens into a scientific folio. Earth is a real object at the middle of an otherwise quiet page. A partial elliptical type horizon carries **“Find what is changing.”**, rather than two static blocked-out words. A readable front sentence explains regional NASA/NOAA records and significance testing. The existing geographic grid remains a subtle instrument of orientation.

First screen: warm charcoal, large ivory editorial lettering, precise small interface sans, a single vermilion action. The globe gets natural light and real land texture rather than a luminous blue cutout. Header stays NASA/project left, Features/AirLLM right. The glass prompt is a short smoked-ivory capsule below the globe; typing a place brings one marker and a readable place label into alignment. No constellation particles, false telemetry, coordinate streams, or game objectives.

On submission, the question is retained as a folio title while the backend stages acquire → check → test appear as actual progress. Results open directly below: full-width chart, a side-note verdict, and a source index. No repeated feature-card grid. A compact three-step strip carries the educational story before results exist.

DigiTwin changes the folio's light: the same globe rolls upside down, type momentarily defocuses and the ground warms to dark rose. **“A world under your assumptions.”** replaces the observed headline. The prompt selects twin intent, but explicit scenario settings remain the authority. A thin labeled comparison rail visually distinguishes simulated reference from scenario median. Form and chart styling derives from the folio, not a separate dashboard.

AirLLM is a local-computing field guide: a clear question → science backend → local model → checked explanation diagram, official runtime download, copyable commands and a complete fallback explanation. Avoid an animated fake terminal.

## Costs and acceptance

Keep one renderer; no postprocessing, no second scene, no type-mesh rebuild each frame. The type horizon is CSS/SVG text geometry, not WebGL. Only one entrance and the reversible world transformation. Preserve zero paused/offscreen draws, bounded DPR, keyboard rotation, 44px controls and readable text on 320px screens. The type horizon is the highest-risk element: use a still asymmetrical composition on touch devices rather than shrinking its text.

## Debate: why I support Living Atlas with conditions

The pale Field Atlas proposal is a stronger complete product identity than a second dark immersive stage. It gives scientific records, scenario forms and local-runtime instructions one readable world. It also creates a genuine replacement for the rejected design.

My challenge: Newsreader and ivory alone can make this look like an environmental magazine or travel site. The actual task must be visible at first glance; sources, variable identity and explicit simulation settings must remain close to the working surface. Do not add book-like decoration, vintage map textures, postage stamps or faux handwritten annotations. Do not turn Earth into a framed illustration. Natural cloud/terrain detail, a real interactive sphere and a compact glass question handle should supply the contemporary technical character.

Recommendation: choose Living Atlas as the coherent world, preserve immersive continuity as its interaction principle, and reject both the cockpit costume and the toy-game onboarding. Do not blend the charcoal proposal into half the observed screens.
