- [Earth Nullschool](https://earth.nullschool.net/): globe-centered working surface; date, units, source, grid and animation controls remain close to the data. Borrow contextual control, reject expert-density navigation.

- [Google Earth Studio](https://www.google.com/earth/studio/): describes geographic motion through concrete actions. [NASA Eyes](https://eyes.nasa.gov/apps/earth/) URL verified but no readable interface, so no invented observations.
- [Awwwards performance publication](https://www.awwwards.com/brainfood-mobile-performance-vol3.pdf): motion is bounded by frame budget and easing. Gallery requests timed out; no unverified award claims.

## Original direction

A bright digital atlas: Earth is the instrument, the question its handle, evidence the open sheet underneath. Reject another black globe behind two giant lines.

Glacier chalk `#EDF3ED`, navy ink `#102B33`, cobalt `#244BFF`, signal coral `#FF785E`. Schibsted Grotesk display/body; IBM Plex Mono only for units, dates and code. One hairline system, generous whitespace, round small actions and square evidence sections.

The complete Earth occupies the centered 55–60% of the hero. Oversized cobalt **“WHAT IS / CHANGING?”** sits behind its silhouette, with the first phrase always visible. A front-layer sentence explains regional NASA-data trend investigation. Small NASA mark left; Features/AirLLM right.

A translucent near-white prompt capsule sits below Earth, with navy text and a coral circular send control. A small DigiTwin switch follows. Actual location/topic resolution becomes a compact reading, never fake telemetry. A marker and one short leader connect question to place. Topic tint touches a thin ring, never pretends to be measured heat.

DigiTwin preserves the canvas and rolls Earth 180°. Ground warms to apricot `#F4D8CF`; type becomes cranberry `#6C2541`. A second thin globe outline identifies hypothetical context. Heading: **“A world under assumptions.”** Every result remains simulated. Returning restores observed state.

The entire product shares the atlas-sheet composition: region index tabs, wide chart, a narrow verdict/units/caveats/source column; explicit variable → baseline → assumptions → result twin forms; AirLLM as a concise local-runtime field guide with official downloads and copyable commands. One short ask → acquire → test strip replaces repetitive feature cards.

Mobile stacks the sheet, preserves the full globe and readable heading, 16px input and 44px controls. Keep the single scene, capped DPR, demand rendering and lazy charts.

Weakness: daylight Earth contrast requires care; less immediate spectacle than an exhibit. Acceptance: clear task in ten seconds, no fake numbers, one canvas across20 toggles, zero paused/offscreen rendering, no overflow320–1440px.
